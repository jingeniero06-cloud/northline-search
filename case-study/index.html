Case study · Bay Property Management Group

What a full crawl found that an automated report missed.

Bay Property Management Group manages residential rentals across Maryland, Pennsylvania, Washington DC and Northern Virginia, on a WordPress site with thousands of pages. Every figure below is from the audit delivered in October 2026 and reflects the site at that time.

Starting point. An automated report had scored the site 86/100 and stated there were zero errors and zero redirects. Our crawl reached 4,758 URLs and found broken links, redirect chains, live placeholder text and a mobile page that took over 15 seconds to show its main image.

What we found

1. Pricing placeholders and contradicting fees

The live fees page displayed "Confirm before publish" in four table cells. Renewal and management fees on city pages disagreed with the fees page, and one FAQ (also in its FAQ schema) read "a one-time startup fee of a one-time onboarding fee". AI assistants quote this kind of text directly to prospective customers.

2. A broken phone link on every page

The footer "call us" button pointed to http://(xxx)%20xxx-xxxx, a web address, instead of a tel: link. On mobile, every tap went to an error page across all 2,120 pages.

3. Mobile speed: 15.1-second LCP

Mobile Lighthouse performance ranged from 33 to 53. A caching plugin lazy-loaded the hero background image, so the largest element painted last. Three separate Google tag libraries (~530 KB) and reCAPTCHA (356 KB) loaded on every page.

4. AI crawlers blocked at the origin

The CDN served cached pages to every bot, but uncached URLs, including all blog posts, llms.txt and the sitemaps, returned 403 to one major AI crawler. Under fast crawling, the origin showed a "One moment, please..." page to every user agent, including Googlebot.

5. Out-of-date legal content

The Maryland landlord-tenant guide still described a two-month security-deposit limit that Maryland replaced with a one-month cap in October 2024.

6. Index hygiene

  • 14 redirecting and 42 noindex URLs listed in the XML sitemap.
  • A theme demo post, a "-duplicate" page and two ad landing copies indexable.
  • Two county contact buttons returning 404, plus 5 two-hop redirect chains.
  • Discontinued location pages redirecting to the homepage.
  • Internal links carrying UTM tracking parameters.

Takeaway. Most of the highest-impact problems were cheap template and configuration fixes. They were invisible to a sampled report and obvious to a full crawl with live re-checks.
